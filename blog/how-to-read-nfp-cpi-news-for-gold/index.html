How to Read NFP, CPI and Other News Data When Trading Gold

Markets do not react to a number, they react to the surprise: the gap between the actual figure and what traders expected. On US jobs-report Fridays gold's average daily range has been about 1.24 times a normal day, so these are the days to respect, not to gamble on.

The releases that move gold

Gold pays no interest, so it reacts to anything that changes what traders expect from interest rates and the US dollar. A handful of scheduled releases do most of that work:

  • NFP (Non-Farm Payrolls). The monthly US jobs report from the Bureau of Labor Statistics, normally released at 8:30 a.m. Eastern on the first Friday of the month. It carries the headline job gain, the unemployment rate and wage growth.
  • CPI (Consumer Price Index). The monthly US inflation report, also from the Bureau of Labor Statistics, usually around the middle of the month. Traders read the headline number and the core number that strips out food and energy.
  • FOMC. The Federal Reserve's rate-setting committee meets eight times a year. The decision and statement come at 2:00 p.m. Eastern, with a press conference afterwards. The words can move markets as much as the rate.
  • PCE inflation. The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, published by the Bureau of Economic Analysis.
  • Weekly jobless claims, PMI surveys, GDP. Smaller movers, but they feed the same story about growth, inflation and rates.

Why gold reacts: the simple version

Hotter inflation or a stronger job market can push traders to expect higher interest rates for longer. Higher rates raise the cost of holding a metal that pays nothing and tend to lift the dollar, and gold is priced in dollars. So a strong report tends to pressure gold, and a weak one tends to help it.

It is a tendency, not a rule

Hot inflation can also support gold as an inflation hedge, and markets sometimes move the opposite way for an hour before reversing. What matters is not the headline but how it compares with what traders expected.

The three numbers on every release

Every calendar shows three figures for each release. Learn to read them as a set:

Forecast (consensus)The median guess of economists before the releaseThis is already in the price. It is the baseline.
ActualThe figure the government publishesOn its own it means little.

An example with made-up numbers

Say the forecast for job growth is 150,000 and the report shows 210,000. That is a strong upside surprise, so traders might expect higher rates for longer, which tends to weigh on gold. If the report shows 150,000, exactly as forecast, there is no surprise and the move can be small, even if 150,000 sounds strong. (These numbers are an illustration, not a real release.)

What 16 years of data says about jobs-report days

The jobs report lands on the first Friday of the month, so we looked at every first Friday since 2012, 169 days. On those days gold's average high-to-low range was 1.78% against 1.43% on all other days, about 1.24 times as wide. Since 2024 the average range on those Fridays was $77 versus $68 on other days.

75% of those Fridays had a range wider than the typical day. The rest were ordinary. A news day is not guaranteed to be a big day, which is exactly why people lose money guessing.

A routine for news days

  1. Know the time in your own time zone. US releases at 8:30 a.m. Eastern are 13:30 UTC in winter and 12:30 UTC in summer.
  2. Write down the forecast before the release. You cannot judge a surprise without it.
  3. Expect wider spreads and slippage. Prices can jump past your stop. Plan the size for that.
  4. Do not trade the first seconds. The first move is often a reaction to the headline and is reversed once traders read the details.
  5. If your system is not built for news, stand aside. Skipping a day costs nothing. A bad fill can cost a week.
  6. Review afterwards. Note forecast, actual, and what gold did in the next hour and the next day. Your own notes beat any rule of thumb.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the headline only and ignoring wages, revisions and the details.
  • Treating a number as good or bad without the consensus.
  • Adding to a losing position to be right about the release.
  • Using full position size on a day when volatility is higher than usual.

Frequently asked questions

What time is the NFP release?

Normally 8:30 a.m. Eastern on the first Friday of the month: 13:30 UTC in winter and 12:30 UTC in summer. Always check the official calendar, because holidays can move it.

Is a strong NFP bad for gold?

It tends to be, because a strong job market can raise rate expectations and the dollar. But the reaction depends on the surprise versus the forecast, and the move can reverse.

How much more does gold move on jobs-report days?

In our data from 2012, first Fridays had an average daily range of 1.78% against 1.43% on other days, about 1.24 times as wide.

Should I trade the news?

Only if you have a tested plan for wider spreads and fast reversals. Many traders do better by waiting for the dust to settle.
