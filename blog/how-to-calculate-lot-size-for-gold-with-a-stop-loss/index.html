How to Calculate Lot Size for Gold From Your Stop Loss

Lot size = amount you risk / (stop distance in dollars x 100) on a broker where one lot of gold is 100 ounces. For a 10,000 account risking 1% (100) with a 5-dollar stop, the size is 100 / (5 x 100) = 0.2 lots.

Size the trade from the stop, not from the feeling

Most blown accounts have one thing in common: the lot size was picked first and the stop was an afterthought. Reverse it. Decide how much you can lose, find where the trade is proved wrong, then let those two numbers set the size.

  1. Amount at risk = account size x risk percent. On 10,000 at 1% that is 100.
  2. Stop distance = the number of dollars between entry and stop, in the gold price. If you buy at 4,000.00 with a stop at 3,995.00, the distance is 5.
  3. Lot size = amount at risk / (stop distance x 100). Here that is 100 / (5 x 100) = 0.20 lots.
  4. Round down to your broker's lot step, never up.

The factor 100 comes from the contract size of 100 ounces per lot, so each dollar of movement is $100 per lot. If your broker uses a different contract size, use that number instead.

Choose the stop first

Put the stop where the idea is wrong, not where the lot size feels comfortable. If the proper stop makes the lot too small to be worth taking, skip the trade. That is the system doing its job.

Check the whole account, not one trade

If three trades are open at 1% each, you can lose 3% on one bad day. Add the risk of all open trades and keep it below the figure you chose in advance. Two trades in the same direction on gold are largely one idea: see correlation and overexposure.

Frequently asked questions

What risk per trade is sensible?

Many professional risk guides use between 0.25% and 2% of the account per trade. Smaller is safer; the right number depends on your drawdown tolerance.

Should the stop depend on volatility?

Yes. A fixed dollar stop that was wide last year can be tight today. See how a stop can be set from the average daily range in our lesson on stop-loss placement.

What if my broker's lot step is 0.01?

Round the result down to the nearest 0.01. Rounding up increases your risk above the figure you chose.
