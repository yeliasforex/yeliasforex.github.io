FOMC Decision Day: How to Handle a Rate Decision as a Gold Trader

The Federal Open Market Committee meets eight times a year and announces its rate decision, usually at 2:00 pm Eastern, followed by a press conference. Gold can swing in both directions within minutes. A risk-first approach is to know the time, reduce or avoid new trades beforehand, and expect wide spreads.

YeliasPublished 18 November 20262 min read

What the FOMC is and when it speaks

The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) is the part of the US central bank that sets the interest-rate target. It holds eight scheduled meetings a year. The decision and a written statement are usually released at 2:00 pm Eastern time, with a press conference by the chair about half an hour later. Dates are published in advance by the Federal Reserve.

What usually happens to gold

  • Before: markets often quieten, then positions are adjusted. Spreads can widen shortly before the release.
  • At the statement: a sharp jump, often in one direction, followed by a reversal. The first move is frequently not the lasting one.
  • During the press conference: new swings as the chair answers questions.
  • After: the real direction can take hours to settle.

Gold is sensitive because rates change the cost of holding a non-yielding asset. The surprise matters more than the decision: if the market expected a move and it came, the reaction can be small. See how to read news data.

  1. Check the calendar for the date and time, in your broker's time zone.
  2. Decide your plan the day before, not in the minute it happens.
  3. Check open trades. Know where each stop is and what it costs if slippage doubles the loss.
  4. Do not widen a stop in the minutes before the release hoping to survive it.
Stops are not guaranteed

In very fast moves an order can be filled well away from its price. Always size positions so that even a worse-than-planned fill is survivable.

Frequently asked questions

How many FOMC meetings are there each year?

Eight regularly scheduled meetings, with the dates published in advance by the Federal Reserve. Extra meetings can be called in exceptional times.

Should I avoid trading gold on FOMC days?

Many traders reduce or avoid new positions around the release. Whether to do so is a risk decision you should make in advance.
