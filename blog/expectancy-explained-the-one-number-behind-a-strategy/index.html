Expectancy Explained: The One Number Behind Any Strategy

Expectancy is the average result per trade: win rate times average win, minus loss rate times average loss. Measured in units of risk (R), a strategy that wins 40% of the time at 2-to-1 earns +0.2R per trade, while one that wins 60% at 0.5-to-1 loses 0.1R per trade.

YeliasPublished 19 November 20262 min read

The formula

Expectancy = (win rate x average win) - (loss rate x average loss). If you express wins and losses in multiples of what you risked (R), a loss is -1R and a win is +R. Then the expectancy is p x R - (1 - p), where p is the win rate.

Positive expectancy means that, if the future behaves like the sample, the strategy earns money on average. Negative means it does not, however good it feels.

Three things that make expectancy misleading

  • Small samples. Twenty trades say very little. The result of a short run is mostly luck. You want a few hundred trades before you trust the average.
  • Costs left out. Spread, commission and slippage come off every trade. A strategy with +0.05R before costs can be negative after them.
  • Changing conditions. An expectancy measured in a calm market can break in a wild one. This is why we test on many years, as in our lesson on backtest versus live results.
  1. Record every trade in R, including the ones you wish you had not taken.
  2. Compute the average of all of them after costs.
  3. Look at the spread of results, not just the average: how large were the worst few?
  4. Wait for enough trades before changing anything based on the number.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good expectancy?

Anything reliably above zero after costs is positive. Values like +0.1R to +0.3R are common for sound systems; much higher numbers from a short sample are usually luck or an error.

Why can I lose money with positive expectancy?

Expectancy is an average. Any single trade, and any short run of trades, can lose. Position size decides whether you survive the bad stretches.

Is expectancy the same as profit factor?

They are related. Profit factor is gross profit divided by gross loss; expectancy is the average per trade. Both should be read together with the drawdown.
