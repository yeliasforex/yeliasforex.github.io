Backtest vs Live: Why EA Results Differ and How to Check Them

A backtest is a simulation of the past, and every shortcut in it flatters the result. The only evidence that counts is a live account, on the same broker you will use, tracked by someone else. A good backtest is where an EA's story starts, not where it ends.

YeliasPublished 4 November 20262 min read

What a backtest really is

A backtest replays an EA's rules over historical prices and adds up the trades. It is useful: it shows whether an idea ever worked and how big the losing streaks were. It is also easy to get wrong, and almost every error makes the result look better than reality.

Ten reasons live results differ

How to check an EA properly

  1. Demand a live record on a third-party tracker such as Myfxbook, on a real account, with the broker named. We publish ours: see our live account.
  2. Compare like with like. Same broker, same account type, same symbol. Our EA is supported only on Fusion Markets and IC Markets Raw accounts for this reason.
  3. Run it on a demo account first for at least a month and compare trade by trade with the back-test over the same dates.
  4. Check the trade count. A back-test of 30 trades says almost nothing; look for hundreds.
  5. Look at drawdown, not only profit. Ask how deep it went and how long it lasted, in testing and live.
  6. Look for a stable curve across years, not one lucky period.
  7. Ask what happens when it stops working. There should be a rule for that.
  8. Start small. Use the smallest plan or lot size until live results match expectations.
What to compare

Take the same date range in the back-test and on a demo or live account. Compare number of trades, win rate, average win and loss, and maximum drawdown. Small gaps are normal. Large, one-sided gaps are a warning.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my EA live result worse than the backtest?

Usually a mix of wider spreads, slippage, execution delay, imperfect test data and settings tuned too closely to the past. Compare trade by trade on the same broker to find which.

How long should I forward test an EA?

At least a month on a demo account, and ideally until it has produced a few dozen trades, so you can compare with the back-test over the same dates.

What is overfitting?

Tuning an EA's settings until it looks perfect on past data. It then fits the past's random noise and tends to fail on new prices.

Is a backtest useless?

No. It shows whether an idea ever worked and how bad the losing streaks were. It is just not enough on its own.
