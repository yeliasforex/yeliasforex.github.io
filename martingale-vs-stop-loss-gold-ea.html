Martingale vs a stop loss in a gold EA: why it matters

Many gold EAs look great on a chart and then lose most of an account in a single bad day. The cause is usually the same: they add size after a loss (martingale), stack more trades against the market (grid), or never close a losing trade at all. A fixed stop loss on every trade is the opposite design.

How martingale works

After a loss, the EA doubles the next trade. The idea is that one win will repay all the earlier losses plus a small profit. It works until the first long losing streak. The arithmetic is simple:

Losses in a rowSize of the next tradeTotal already lost
12 units1 unit
24 units3 units
38 units7 units
416 units15 units
532 units31 units
664 units63 units

Six losses in a row is not rare for any strategy. At that point the EA needs a 64-unit trade, and the account may not have the margin for it. The win it was chasing is worth one unit.

Grid and averaging down

A grid opens new trades at fixed steps against the price, and averaging down adds to a losing position. Both lower the average entry price, so a small bounce looks like a win. Gold often trends hard, so the position grows exactly when the market keeps moving against it. Results look smooth for weeks, then one trend wipes them out.

What a stop loss changes

  • The loss is known in advance. Each trade can lose a fixed, small part of the account.
  • Losing streaks stay survivable. Six losses in a row cost six small losses, not a 64-unit trade.
  • The trade-off is real. A stop loss means many small losses and a lower win rate than recovery-style systems. The edge has to come from winners being larger than losers, not from never losing.

What to check in any EA

  1. Ask whether lot size ever increases after a loss.
  2. Ask whether it ever opens more than one position in the same direction as a "recovery".
  3. Check that every position has a stop loss set when it opens (look at the trades on a live or demo account).
  4. In the backtest, look at the largest floating loss, not only the closed-trade drawdown.

Questions people ask

What is martingale in trading?

Martingale means increasing the size of the next trade after a loss, usually doubling it, so one win recovers the earlier losses. It fails when a losing streak is longer than the account can fund.

Is a grid EA the same as martingale?

They are related. A grid opens extra trades at fixed steps against the price. Both build bigger positions as the market moves against them, which is why both can fail badly in a strong trend.

Why is a stop loss safer?

A stop loss caps the loss of each trade at a known amount, so a losing streak costs a few small losses instead of an account-sized position. The cost is a lower win rate and more small losses.
