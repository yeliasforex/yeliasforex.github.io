Drawdown Recovery Maths: Why a 50% Loss Needs a 100% Gain

A loss is always smaller in percent than the gain needed to undo it. Lose 20% and you need +25% to get back to even; lose 50% and you need +100%. The formula is gain needed = 1 / (1 - loss) - 1, and it is why limiting drawdown matters more than chasing returns.

YeliasPublished 6 November 20262 min read

The asymmetry nobody feels until it happens

Say an account falls from 10,000 to 8,000. That is a 20% loss. To get back to 10,000 it has to grow from 8,000, and 2,000 on top of 8,000 is 25%. The loss and the gain are measured against different starting points, so they are never equal.

The formula is short: gain needed = 1 / (1 - loss) - 1, with the loss written as a fraction. A 20% loss is 0.20, so 1 / 0.80 - 1 = 0.25. Everything else in this lesson follows from that one line.

Why this changes how you trade

  • Small drawdowns are cheap, big ones are expensive. A 10% drawdown needs about 11% to repair. A 40% drawdown needs about 67%, which can take years.
  • Surviving matters more than winning big. An account that never falls below -20% can use almost every good year it gets. An account that falls -50% spends its next good years only getting back to start.
  • Position size is the control. You cannot control whether the next trade wins, but you can control how much one loss costs. See position sizing and risk of ruin.

A practical rule of thumb

Decide in advance the worst drawdown you can live with, in percent, and write it down. Then work backwards: with a given risk per trade, how many losses in a row would reach that number? If five losses in a row would do it, you are sized too big for that tolerance. Many traders find this the most useful single question in risk management.

The one-line takeaway

You do not need to avoid losses. You need to avoid the large ones, because the maths of recovery punishes them more than anything else.

Frequently asked questions

How much do I need to gain after a 30% loss?

About 42.9%. The formula is 1 / (1 - 0.30) - 1, which is 0.4286.

Is a 20% drawdown normal for a trading robot?

It depends on the strategy and the risk settings, which is why a robot's maximum drawdown should always be read next to its returns and its time period. A drawdown you cannot tolerate is too big for you, whatever the average.

Does this apply to profits too?

Yes, the same asymmetry works in reverse: percent changes compound, so a +25% year followed by a -20% year leaves you exactly where you started.
