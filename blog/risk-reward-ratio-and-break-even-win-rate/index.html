Risk-Reward Ratio and the Win Rate You Need to Break Even

The win rate you need to break even is 1 / (1 + R), where R is your average reward divided by your average risk. At 1-to-1 you must win half your trades, at 2-to-1 only a third, and at 0.5-to-1 two thirds. Costs push the real number higher.

YeliasPublished 11 November 20262 min read

Two numbers that only make sense together

A trader says: 'I win 70% of my trades.' Another says: 'I only win 35%.' Without more information you cannot tell who is doing better. What matters is the size of the average win compared with the average loss. That ratio is called R, reward to risk.

If you risk 100 to make 200, R is 2. If you risk 200 to make 100, R is 0.5. The break-even win rate, before any costs, is 1 / (1 + R).

The trap on both sides

  • High win rate, small wins, big losses. Many traders feel safe because they win often, then one large loss erases weeks. The break-even rate at 0.5 : 1 is two thirds, so a 65% win rate is a losing system.
  • Low win rate, big wins. This style is statistically sound but psychologically hard: long strings of small losses test discipline. Our lesson on handling a losing streak shows how long streaks really get.
  • Neither is better in itself. The product of win rate and reward-to-risk is what counts. See expectancy explained.
Costs change the picture

On a short-term gold strategy the spread can be a large share of a small stop. A system that breaks even on paper can lose after costs. Always use after-cost results when you compare strategies.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good risk-reward ratio?

There is no universal answer. A higher ratio lets you be right less often, but it usually means targets that price reaches less often. The two have to be judged together through expectancy.

Can I just set a 3 : 1 target and be profitable?

No. Price reaches a far target less often than a near one, so a wider target also lowers your win rate. The only test is the result over many trades.

Does a high win rate mean a good robot?

Not by itself. Ask what the average win and the average loss are, and what the worst drawdown was.
