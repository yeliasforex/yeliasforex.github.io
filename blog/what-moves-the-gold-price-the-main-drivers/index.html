What Moves the Gold Price? The Main Drivers Explained

Gold has no yield, so it is sensitive to interest rates and the US dollar, to inflation expectations, to fear and uncertainty, and to buying by central banks and investors. These forces change in strength over time and sometimes pull in opposite directions, so no single driver predicts the price.

YeliasPublished 9 November 20262 min read

Why gold is different

Gold pays no interest and no dividend. Its price comes from what people are willing to pay to hold it. That is why the forces below matter: they change the cost of holding gold or the wish to hold it.

DriverUsual logicWhy it is not a rule
Interest rates and yieldsHigher rates make non-yielding gold less attractive, lower rates help itMarkets move on what rates are expected to do, not what they are now
The US dollarGold is priced in dollars, so a stronger dollar often weighs on itThe link weakens or reverses at times
Inflation expectationsGold is seen as a store of value when prices riseIt depends on whether central banks are expected to respond with higher rates
Fear and uncertaintyInvestors buy gold as a refuge in a crisisIn a sudden panic, even gold can be sold to raise cash
Central-bank and investor demandLarge buyers support pricesFlows change slowly and are reported with delay

Scheduled events turn the drivers into moves

Many of these forces show up on the calendar: inflation reports, jobs data and central-bank meetings. The surprise, not the number itself, usually moves the price. See how to read NFP, CPI and other news data and, for the size of normal moves, how much gold moves in a day.

How to use this knowledge

  1. Know the calendar. Be aware of the big releases and decisions before you open a trade.
  2. Ask what is already expected. A number that matches expectations rarely moves the market.
  3. Do not trade a story without a plan. Even a right view needs an entry, a stop and a size.
  4. Expect the dominant driver to change. Last year's explanation may not work this year.
Beware the confident explanation

After any big move, someone will give a neat reason. Explanations written after the fact are easy; forecasts made before the move are hard. That is why we score every forecast in public.

Frequently asked questions

Does gold always rise when the dollar falls?

No. The relationship is a tendency and it changes over time. Both can move together for periods.

Is gold a good hedge against inflation?

Over long periods it has often held value, but over months and years the link to inflation is loose and depends on interest rates.

What is the biggest driver today?

It changes. Looking at which driver the market is focused on, usually visible in news and rate expectations, is more useful than assuming a fixed one.
