AI Trading Bots and AI Agents: What They Can and Cannot Do

AI is very good at reading, explaining and organising information, and much weaker at predicting prices. We tested a careful data-driven forecaster on 3,595 past days of gold: it was honest about ranges, but its edge on direction was just 0.16% better than a simple long-run average.

Three very different things get called AI

TypeWhat it doesWhere it is strongWhere it breaks
Rule-based robot (EA)Follows fixed rules written by a personDiscipline, speed, never tired or scaredRules can stop working when markets change
Machine learning modelLearns patterns from past dataFinding structure in large datasetsOverfits: learns the past's noise, fails on new data
LLM agent (language model)Reads and writes text, can call toolsSummaries, explanations, monitoring news, boring choresWeak at precise numbers, can sound confident while wrong

What AI genuinely does well in trading

  • Explaining. Turning a price move or a trade into plain language, quickly and at any hour.
  • Organising information. Summarising news, tracking many markets, flagging unusual conditions.
  • Checking and monitoring. Watching a system for errors that a person would miss at 3 a.m.
  • Testing ideas faster. Writing and running experiments, which is how we ran the test below.

What it cannot do (yet)

  • See the future. Markets are noisy and competitive. A model with a small edge is common, a model with a large stable edge is extremely rare.
  • Guarantee profit. Any seller who says otherwise is selling something else.
  • Reason reliably with exact numbers. Language models can invent a figure and state it with confidence, which is why numbers should come from code, not from the model.
  • Replace risk management. The most advanced model still needs position sizing and a stop.

A test we ran on ourselves

To check how much a careful, data-driven approach could find, we built a forecaster for gold. For each day it finds past days with a similar volatility, trend and position in the recent range, looks at what happened next, and turns that into probabilities. Then we tested it on 3,595 past days using only information that was available on the morning of each day, so it could not peek.

The honest finding

With 16 years of data and a careful method, daily direction was almost unpredictable. What the model did well was say how far gold is likely to move and how likely it is to touch a level. That is useful for risk and stops, but it is not a crystal ball. See our live scorecard for how every forecast is scored in public.

Ten questions to ask any AI trading product

  1. Is there a verified live track record on a third-party tracker, on a real account, with a track record longer than a few months?
  2. Are the back-tests on real tick data with realistic spreads and slippage?
  3. Was it tested on data it never saw (out-of-sample or forward-tested)?
  4. What is the maximum drawdown in testing and in live trading, and is it on the same account type you will use?
  5. How many trades is the result based on? Under a hundred proves nothing.
  6. What happens when the market changes? Does it stop, adapt, or keep trading blindly?
  7. Who holds the money? It should always stay in your own broker account.
  8. What does the AI actually do? Predict, execute, or just explain?
  9. Is anything guaranteed? If yes, walk away.
  10. Can you watch it live? A seller who hides the live account is telling you something.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI predict the gold price?

Not reliably. In our walk-forward test over 3,595 days a careful model had almost no edge on direction (0.16% better than a simple average) but gave honest ranges.

What is the difference between an EA and an AI trading bot?

An EA follows fixed rules written by a person. An AI bot may use a machine-learning model or a language model. Many products use the label AI for what is really a rule-based EA.

Is an AI trading agent safe?

Only with strict limits on position size, a stop on every trade, and funds that stay in your own account. Language-model agents can state wrong numbers confidently, so they should not set risk.
